The World

Nothing's Impossible.
It Just Means Someone
Rich Has Made the Door Ugly.

Nasyk is a thief. Small, blue, very good at climbing things and taking things that don't belong to her. She works with crews she cannot quite afford to trust, in cities that would cheerfully see her dead, for money that is — for once — clean.

Every book in the Velvet Knife series is a heist inside a different city — each one a world with its own machinery of power, its own ugly door, its own reason you'd be a fool to try. The price is always worse than the plan. The plan always falls apart. Nasyk always comes out the other side.

But the doors aren't the whole story. Beneath them runs the Sixfold Claim — six houses, in six of the oldest cities, each owning a piece of what it means to be a person: who may bind you, who may file you gone, who may decide you ever happened, who names you, what you can inherit, and what is still coming for you. Nasyk isn't selling her luck for gold. She's tearing down the machinery that owns people, one city at a time.

And she didn't choose any of it. Three witches have been arranging the doors in front of her since before she knew the game had a name — a mirror merchant who sells what you mistake yourself for, a scroll keeper who writes in still-wet ink long past its date, and a sea witch with salt on her breath. At first they seemed like patrons. Increasingly, they seem like the only thing that might keep her alive.

Street-level fantasy. No prophecy. No chosen one. Just a very small woman, a very ugly door, and the one crew that might — might — be good enough.

"I'm not noble. I'm not kind. I'm just the one who comes through the door that was meant to stay shut."
— Nasyk, The Silver Vow

The Books

The Velvet Knife · Book Two

The Silver Vow

Where debt is inherited like a disease.

In a city where debt is passed down like a disease, one thief means to steal the thing that makes a promise binding. Vael Corran runs on owing — its merchant houses can bind a child to a bond their grandfather signed, and the Witnesses make certain no debt is ever forgotten.

Nasyk wants the Silver Vow. For the money, yes. But also because she has spent her whole life running from the thing this city does to a hundred thousand people at once. The plan is impossible. The cost is worse.

The Velvet Knife · Book Three

The House That Keeps the Names

The truth can free the dead. It can also burn a city down.

In a metropolis ruled by memorial houses and professional mourners, a person only truly existed if the death-records said so — and the powerful had been erasing the murdered for generations and taking their wealth.

Nasyk and her crew mean to steal a black vessel from the heart of the death-house and give the names back. Some debts aren't paid in gold. Some are paid in names.

The Velvet Knife · Book Four

The Drowned Masque

In Zimira, your name is your currency. Lose it, and you lose everything.

Every citizen of this canal city pays to be kept — registered, remembered, maintained in the Archivist's great ledger of names. The more you're seen, the more you matter. Stop paying, and the system quietly erases you. Not your body. Just your record. Your reputation. Your right to exist on paper.

Nasyk has six weeks until the Grand Masquerade. The Archivist's register has noticed her. Her own name is beginning to disappear. Every mask hides a face. Only one of them is the job.

The Author

Kennedy
MacEoghain

Kennedy MacEoghain writes street-level fantasy fiction about thieves, cities, and the ugly machinery of power. The Velvet Knife series follows one small, blue, very capable thief across six impossible jobs in six impossible cities.

Also the author of the Beyond Andromeda science fiction saga — a sweeping epic of deep time, forbidden love, and a people raised across four billion years to ask the one question that matters.
